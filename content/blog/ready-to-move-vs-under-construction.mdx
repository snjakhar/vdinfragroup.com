One of the first decisions every buyer faces is whether to buy a finished home or book one that is still being built. Neither is always better. It depends on your budget, your timeline and how much uncertainty you are comfortable with.

## The case for ready-to-move

- **What you see is what you get.** You can walk through the actual home, check the finishes and see the view.
- **No waiting.** You can move in, or start earning rent, soon after registration.
- **Lower delivery risk.** The building is complete and the occupancy certificate is in hand.
- **Taxes.** GST generally does not apply to completed homes that have an occupancy certificate. Confirm the current rules with your tax adviser.

The trade-off is price. Finished homes usually cost more per square foot than the same builder's new launches, and the best units are often already sold.

## The case for under-construction

- **Lower entry price.** Pre-launch and early-stage prices are typically the lowest a project will ever see.
- **Better choice.** You can pick your floor, facing and layout.
- **Staggered payments.** Construction-linked plans spread the cost over the build period.

The trade-off is time and risk. You may pay rent and EMI together for a while, and you depend on the builder delivering on schedule.

## How to reduce the risk of buying early

1. Buy only RERA-registered projects, and read the registered completion date.
2. Study the builder's record: visit two or three completed projects and talk to residents.
3. Choose a construction-linked payment plan rather than a large upfront payment.
4. Check that the land title and approvals are clear before you book.

## Our view

If you need a home soon or want certainty, choose ready-to-move. If you can wait and want the best price, a new launch from a builder with a proven delivery record can make sense.
